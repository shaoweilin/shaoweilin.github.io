Action-Driven Processes

Action-Driven Processes

  • States \(x \in \mathcal{X}\) and actions \(a \in \mathcal{A}\). At state \(x\), each action \(a\) arrives independently at action rate \(\lambda_{xa}^\beta\), where \(\beta\) is the inverse temperature.
  • The first arrival \(a\) triggers a transition \(x \to y\) with probability \(p_{xay}\).
  • With constant rates and potentials \(\rho_{xa} = \log \lambda_{xa}\), the wait time \(W\) and action \(A\) from state \(x\) have density \[ \log \mathbb{P}[W, A \,\vert\, x] = \beta\,\rho_{xA} - W \sum_{a} e^{\beta \rho_{xa}}. \]
  • Paths \((w_1, a_1, x_1), (w_2, a_2, x_2), \ldots\) have densities that are products of these and of the \(p_{x a y}\).
  • Also known as generalized semi-Markov processes. Spiking networks: one action per neuron.

Lin. Relative Information Theory II: Reinforcement Learning, Ch. 2 (in preparation).

Binary Flip Processes

  • States \(x \in \{0,1\}^d\), the on/off states of \(d\) neurons.
  • Actions \(a_1, \ldots, a_d\), where \(a_i\) flips bit \(i\): \(x \mapsto x^{(i)}\). Transitions are deterministic, \(p_{x a_i y} = \mathbb{1}[y = x^{(i)}]\).
  • Action rates are Boltzmann factors of a pair energy \(E(x, y)\): \[ \lambda_{x,i}^\beta = e^{-\beta E(x, x^{(i)})}. \]
  • The result is a continuous-time Markov chain on \(2^d\) states with generator \[ \Gamma_{x x^{(i)}} = \lambda^\beta_{x,i}, \qquad \Gamma_{xx} = -\sum_i \lambda^\beta_{x,i}, \qquad \Gamma_{xy} = 0 \text{ otherwise}. \]

Energies

  • State energies of arbitrary order, with \(x_S = \prod_{i \in S} x_i\): \[ E_W(x) = -\sum_{\emptyset \neq S \subseteq [d]} w_S\, x_S, \qquad \text{e.g. } E(x) = -\tfrac12 x^\top W x \text{ (using } x_i^2 = x_i). \]
  • Pair energy \(E(x, y) = \frac12\big(E(y) - E(x)\big)\): half the energy change.
  • Local field \(h_i(x) = \sum_{S \ni i} w_S\, x_{S \setminus i}\), which does not depend on \(x_i\). Flipping bit \(i\) lowers the energy by \[ \Delta_i(x) = E(x) - E(x^{(i)}) = (1 - 2x_i)\, h_i(x). \]
  • So the action rates are \(\lambda^\beta_{x,i} = e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2}\): downhill flips are fast, uphill flips are slow.

Detailed Balance

  • For \(y = x^{(i)}\), \[ e^{-\beta E(x)}\, \Gamma_{xy} = e^{-\beta E(x)}\, e^{\beta(E(x) - E(y))/2} = e^{-\beta (E(x) + E(y))/2}, \] which is symmetric in \(x\) and \(y\).
  • So the Gibbs law \(\pi_\beta(x) \propto e^{-\beta E(x)}\) satisfies detailed balance, \(\pi_\beta(x)\,\Gamma_{xy} = \pi_\beta(y)\,\Gamma_{yx}\), and the chain is reversible.
  • Summing over \(x\) gives \(\pi_\beta\, \Gamma = 0\): the Gibbs law is a stationary distribution of the flip process.
  • The factor \(\frac12\) splits each energy change evenly between the forward and backward rates.
  • Question. What happens as \(\beta \to \infty\)?

Dynamics

Probabilistic Dynamics

  • Master equation. The law \(p_t\) of the state evolves by \[ \frac{d}{dt} p_t = p_t\, \Gamma, \qquad p_t = p_0\, e^{\Gamma t}. \]
  • For \(\beta < \infty\), every flip has a positive rate and the hypercube is connected, so \(\pi_\beta\) is the unique stationary distribution and \(p_t \to \pi_\beta\) from any \(p_0\).
  • Simulation. From state \(x\):
    1. wait for time \(W \sim \mathrm{Exp}\big(\sum_i \lambda^\beta_{x,i}\big)\);
    2. flip bit \(i\) with probability \(\lambda^\beta_{x,i} \big/ \sum_j \lambda^\beta_{x,j}\).
  • Uniformization. Choose \(\lambda \geq \max_x \sum_i \lambda^\beta_{x,i}\). Draw arrivals at rate \(\lambda\), flip bit \(i\) with probability \(\lambda^\beta_{x,i}/\lambda\), and do nothing otherwise. This simulates the same process in discrete steps.

Lin. Relative Information Theory II: Reinforcement Learning, §§2.3-2.4 (in preparation).

Zero-Temperature Dynamics

As \(\beta \to \infty\), with rates \(e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2}\):

  • If \(\max_i \Delta_i(x) > 0\), the total rate diverges and the action probabilities concentrate on \(\arg\max_i \Delta_i(x)\). The steepest energy-lowering bit flips instantly.
  • If \(\max_i \Delta_i(x) < 0\), all rates vanish. The state \(x\) is absorbing: a strict local minimum of \(E\) on the hypercube.
  • Ties \(\Delta_i(x) = 0\) keep rate \(1\), and ties among the maxima share the probability equally.

Wait times disappear: the limit is a deterministic stepped process. Each step lowers \(E\), so the state diagram is acyclic and every path ends at a local minimum.

Hopfield Networks

  • Hopfield update. Pick a neuron \(i\) and set \(x_i \leftarrow H\big(h_i(x)\big)\), where \(H\) is the Heaviside step function.
  • The update changes \(x_i\) exactly when \(\Delta_i(x) > 0\), so it never raises the energy.
  • The zero-temperature flip process has the same fixed points: the strict local minima of \(E\).
  • The schedule differs: Hopfield networks update in a fixed or random order, while the zero-temperature limit always takes the steepest flip.
  • The set of energy-lowering flips at each state depends only on the signs of the \(h_i(x)\). The steepest one also depends on their sizes.

Hopfield. “Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities.” PNAS 79(8): 2554-2558, 1982.

Learning

Relative Information Rate

  • Truth \(Q\): a Markov chain with rates \(\Gamma^Q_{xy}\) and stationary law \(\pi^Q\), e.g. the data.
  • Model \(P\): a Markov chain with rates \(\Gamma^P_{xy}\) and stationary law \(\pi^P\). For the flip process, \(\Gamma^P = \Gamma\) and \(\pi^P = \pi_\beta\).
  • Learn by minimizing the relative information rate of paths, \[ \frac{d}{ds} I_{Q\Vert P}(X_{0..s}) = \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_{y \neq x} \Big[\Gamma^Q_{xy}\log\frac{\Gamma^Q_{xy}}{\Gamma^P_{xy}} - \Gamma^Q_{xy} + \Gamma^P_{xy}\Big]. \]
  • Each summand is a divergence of rates, \(a \log\frac{a}{b} - a + b \geq 0\).
  • The rate is finite only if the truth makes no moves that the model cannot, i.e. \(\Gamma^Q_{xy} = 0\) unless \(y\) is a flip neighbor of \(x\).

Lin. Relative Information Theory II: Reinforcement Learning, Ch. 4 (in preparation).

Derivation

  • Given \(X_0\), the log ratio of path densities on \([0, s]\), with escape rates \(\Gamma^Q_x = \sum_{y \neq x}\Gamma^Q_{xy}\) and \(\Gamma^P_x = -\Gamma^P_{xx}\), is \[ \log \frac{dQ}{dP} = \sum_{\text{jumps } x \to y} \log\frac{\Gamma^Q_{xy}}{\Gamma^P_{xy}} - \int_0^s \big(\Gamma^Q_{X_t} - \Gamma^P_{X_t}\big)\, dt. \]
  • Under \(Q\), jumps \(x \to y\) occur at rate \(\Gamma^Q_{xy}\) while in \(x\). With \(\pi^Q_t\) the law of \(X_t\) under \(Q\), \[ I_{Q\Vert P}(X_{0..s} \,\vert\, X_0) = \int_0^s \sum_x \pi^Q_t(x) \sum_{y \neq x} \Big[\Gamma^Q_{xy}\log\frac{\Gamma^Q_{xy}}{\Gamma^P_{xy}} - \Gamma^Q_{xy} + \Gamma^P_{xy}\Big]\, dt. \]
  • If \(\pi^Q_0 = \pi^Q\) is stationary for \(Q\), then \(\pi^Q_t = \pi^Q\), and this is exactly \(s\) times the rate, for every \(s\).
  • Checked numerically against discretized paths on \(\{0,1\}^3\).

The density ratio is the Girsanov formula for Markov jump processes (check: a textbook reference). Numerical check: scripts/check_ri_rate.py.

Energy Form

  • With model rates \(\Gamma^P_{x x^{(i)}} = e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2}\), up to a constant \(C\) that does not depend on the model, \[ \frac{d}{ds} I = \underbrace{\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i \Gamma^Q_{x x^{(i)}} \big(E(x^{(i)}) - E(x)\big)}_{\text{energy climbed by the true transitions}} \; + \; \underbrace{\sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2}}_{\text{energy flow}} + C. \]
  • The first term rewards energies that make the true transitions downhill.
  • The second term is \(-\sum_x \pi^Q_x \Gamma^P_{xx}\), the total rate at which the model escapes from the data. Minimizing it makes the data stable.

Minimum Energy Flow

  • Suppose the truth never leaves the data: \(\Gamma^Q_{xy} = 0\) for all \(x\) in the support of \(\pi^Q\). The data are fixed points, i.e. memories.
  • Then the relative information rate is the energy flow \[ K_\beta(W) = \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_{i=1}^d e^{\beta (E_W(x) - E_W(x^{(i)}))/2}. \]
  • Minimum energy flow (MEF) minimizes \(K_\beta(W)\). It is convex in \(W\).
  • Its ancestor, minimum probability flow, minimizes the relative information between the data and the data evolved by the model dynamics for an infinitesimal time. Here MEF is a relative information rate of paths.

Temperature of the Model

  • \(E_W\) is linear in \(W\), so \(K_\beta(W) = K_1(\beta W)\). The model temperature is the scale of \(W\).
  • As \(\beta \to \infty\), with \(\Delta_i(x)\) computed from \(E_W\), \[ K_\beta(W) \to \sum_x \pi^Q_x\, \#\{i : \Delta_i(x) = 0\} \quad\text{if every } \Delta_i(x) \leq 0 \text{ on the data}, \] and \(K_\beta(W) \to \infty\) otherwise. So \(K_\beta(W) \to 0\) if and only if every data point is a strict local minimum of \(E_W\).
  • The infimum of \(K_1\) is \(0\), approached at infinity: MEF pushes \(W\) to infinity in the directions that store the data.

A Noisy Truth Regularizes

  • Suppose instead the truth leaves each data point for each neighbor at a small rate \(c > 0\). The relative information rate becomes \[ \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i \Big[ e^{h_{x,i}} - c\, h_{x,i} \Big] + C, \qquad h_{x,i} = \tfrac{\beta}{2}\Delta_i(x). \]
  • Each term \(e^h - c h\) is minimized at \(h = \log c\), a finite value.
  • This is a regularized MEF: the noise rate keeps each energy gap finite. Whether the minimizer over \(W\) is finite depends on the data [open].

Empirical Energy Flow

  • From one long observed path of duration \(T\), with jumps \(x \to y\) and fraction of time \(\hat\pi^Q_x\) spent in each state \(x\), estimate the relative information rate by \[ \frac{1}{T}\sum_{\text{jumps } x \to y} \frac{\beta}{2}\big(E(y) - E(x)\big) + \sum_x \hat\pi^Q_x \sum_i e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2} + C. \]
  • The first term is the energy climbed along the observed jumps. The second is the energy flow out of the states, weighted by holding times.
  • Without jumps, this is MEF with the empirical law \(\hat\pi^Q\).

Glauber Dynamics

Glauber Rates

  • Any rates \(e^{\beta \Delta/2}\, s_{xy}\) with \(s_{xy} = s_{yx}\) satisfy detailed balance for \(\pi_\beta\). Three choices, with \(\Delta = E(x) - E(y)\) and \(\sigma\) the logistic function:
rates \(s_{xy}\) rate \(\beta \to \infty\)
MEF \(1\) \(e^{\beta\Delta/2}\) \(\infty\) if \(\Delta > 0\), \(\ 0\) if \(\Delta < 0\)
Glauber \(1/(2\cosh(\beta\Delta/2))\) \(\sigma(\beta\Delta)\) \(1\) if \(\Delta > 0\), \(\ 0\) if \(\Delta < 0\)
Metropolis \(e^{-\beta\vert\Delta\vert/2}\) \(\min(1, e^{\beta\Delta})\) \(1\) if \(\Delta > 0\), \(\ 0\) if \(\Delta < 0\)
  • Glauber is the Gibbs sampler. Bit \(i\) is redrawn at rate \(1\) with \(\mathbb{P}(x_i = 1) = \sigma(\beta h_i(x))\). Also \(\Gamma^G_{xy} = \Gamma_{xy}/(\Gamma_{xy} + \Gamma_{yx})\).
  • At zero temperature, Glauber rates keep time: each unstable bit flips at rate \(1\). This is the random-order Hopfield network. MEF rates lose time.
  • We keep MEF rates: they are of the form \(\lambda^\beta\), and they give a convex objective.

Glauber. “Time-dependent statistics of the Ising model.” J. Math. Phys. 4(2): 294-307, 1963.
Metropolis, Rosenbluth, Rosenbluth, Teller, Teller. “Equation of state calculations by fast computing machines.” J. Chem. Phys. 21(6): 1087-1092, 1953.

Pseudo-Likelihood

  • For the Gibbs law, \(\mathbb{P}(x_i \,\vert\, x_{-i}) = \sigma(-\beta \Delta_i(x))\). So the negative pseudo-log-likelihood is \[ \mathrm{PL}_\beta(W) = \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i \log\big(1 + e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)}\big). \]
  • Compare MEF, \(K_\beta(W) = \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i e^{\beta \Delta_i(x)/2}\). Both are convex in \(W\), and both are small when the data are deep local minima.
  • \(\sigma(-\beta\Delta_i(x)) = \Gamma^G_{x^{(i)} x}\) is the Glauber rate of flowing back into the data from a neighbor. So pseudo-likelihood sums log-rates of return flows.
  • Question [open]. Is \(\mathrm{PL}_\beta\), up to constants, the relative information rate between a Glauber model and a truth that knocks the data out by one flip and restores it?

Besag. “Statistical analysis of non-lattice data.” The Statistician 24(3): 179-195, 1975.

The Tropical Limit

Base of the Logarithm

  • Put \(b = e^\beta\). Then the action rates are \(b^{\rho}\) with potentials \(\rho\), and the temperature is \(1/\log b\).
  • As \(b \to \infty\), \[ \log_b\big(b^u + b^v\big) \to \max(u, v), \qquad 0 \leq \log_b\big(b^u + b^v\big) - \max(u, v) \leq \log_b 2. \]
  • So in log-base-\(b\) coordinates, \((\mathbb{R}_{>0}, +, \times)\) becomes the tropical semiring \((\mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}, \max, +)\) as \(b \to \infty\).
  • This is Maslov dequantization, with the temperature in the role of Planck’s constant.

Litvinov. “Maslov dequantization, idempotent and tropical mathematics: a brief introduction.” J. Math. Sci. 140: 426-444, 2007.

Energy Convention

  • In energies \(u = -\lambda \log x\) at temperature \(\lambda = 1/\beta\), addition becomes \[ u \oplus_\lambda v = -\lambda \log\big(e^{-u/\lambda} + e^{-v/\lambda}\big) \;\to\; \min(u, v) \quad (\lambda \to 0). \]
  • \(K_\lambda = (\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}, \oplus_\lambda, +)\) is the Maslov semifield: isomorphic to \(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\) for \(\lambda > 0\), and the tropical semifield \(\mathbb{T} = (\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}, \min, +)\) at \(\lambda = 0\).
  • The Gibbs law \(\pi_\beta(x) = e^{-\beta E(x)} \big/ \sum_y e^{-\beta E(y)}\) has energies \[ -\lambda \log \pi_\beta(x) = E(x) - \textstyle\bigoplus_{\lambda,\, y} E(y) \;\to\; E(x) - \min_y E(y), \] a tropical law: energies normalized so that the ground state has energy \(0\).
  • For a partition of the states, each part \(A\) gets the marginal energy \(E(A) = \bigoplus_{\lambda,\, x \in A} E(x) \to \min_{x \in A} E(x)\), and the conditional law on \(A\) has energies \(E(x) - E(A)\).

Lin. Relative Information Theory I: Information Cohomology, §2.6.6 (in preparation).

Zero Temperature Is Tropical

inverse temperature \(\beta = \log b < \infty\) zero temperature \(b = \infty\)
total rate \(\log_b \sum_i b^{\rho_{x,i}}\) \(\max_i \rho_{x,i}\)
action probabilities \(b^{\rho_{x,i}} \big/ \sum_j b^{\rho_{x,j}}\) indicator of \(\arg\max_i \rho_{x,i}\)
some arrival, at a finite rate instant arrival iff \(\max_i \rho_{x,i} > 0\)
Glauber log-rate \(\log_b \sigma(\beta\Delta)\) Metropolis log-rate \(\min(0, \Delta)\)
Gibbs energies \(-\frac{1}{\beta}\log \pi_\beta\) tropical law \(E - \min E\)
marginal energy of a part \(A\): \(\oplus_\lambda\) over \(A\) \(E(A) = \min_{x \in A} E(x)\)
  • The zero-temperature dynamics are the tropical shadow of the flip process.
  • The threshold “arrive iff \(\rho > 0\)” is the firing threshold of a neuron.

MEF Tropicalizes

  • In base \(b = e^\beta\), \[ \log_b K_\beta(W) = \log_b \sum_x \pi^Q_x \sum_i b^{\Delta_i(x)/2} \;\to\; \max_{x \in \mathrm{supp}\, \pi^Q,\ i}\ \tfrac12 \Delta_i(x). \]
  • Tropical MEF is a maximum of linear forms in \(W\): convex and piecewise linear.
  • It is negative exactly when every data point is a strict local minimum of \(E_W\).
  • So its negative set is an open polyhedral cone in parameter space, a union of chambers of a hyperplane arrangement.
  • Similarly, pseudo-likelihood tropicalizes to a hinge loss, \(\log_b(1 + b^{\Delta}) \to \max(0, \Delta)\).

What Is Lost

  • The tropical sum forgets multiplicities: \[ \log_b\big(b^u + b^u\big) = u + \log_b 2 \;\to\; u. \]
  • At zero temperature we lose the number of tied steepest flips and of degenerate minima.
  • This lost correction is the entropy (Connes-Consani). In information cohomology, the class of the Shannon entropy disappears at zero temperature, unless the multiplicities of the ground states are kept, as in layered tropical laws.

Connes, Consani. “Characteristic 1, entropy and the absolute point.” In Noncommutative Geometry, Arithmetic, and Related Topics, 75-139, 2011.

Chambers and Stability

The Arrangement

  • Parameters \(W = (w_S)_{\emptyset \neq S \subseteq [d]}\) form a space of dimension \(2^d - 1\).
  • Each local field \(h_i(x)\), for a neuron \(i\) and the other states \(x_{-i} \in \{0,1\}^{d-1}\), is a linear form in \(W\). Its zero set is a hyperplane: \(d\, 2^{d-1}\) hyperplanes in all.
  • A chamber fixes the signs of all \(h_i(x)\), so it fixes the energy-lowering flips at every state, and hence the fixed points. Walls are ties.
  • Chambers are counted by the characteristic polynomial \(\chi\): there are \(\lvert\chi(-1)\rvert\) of them.
    • \(d = 2\): \(\chi(t) = t^3 - 4t^2 + 6t - 3\), so \(14\) chambers.
    • \(d = 3\): \(12\) hyperplanes in \(\mathbb{R}^7\), so \(1862\) chambers.

Zaslavsky. Facing up to arrangements: face-count formulas for partitions of space by hyperplanes. Mem. AMS 1(154), 1975.
Chamber counts: scripts/flip_arrangement.py.

Two Neurons

  • \(E(x) = -w_1 x_1 - w_2 x_2 - w_{12} x_1 x_2\).
  • Four hyperplanes in \((w_1, w_2, w_{12})\):
    • \(w_1 = 0\) and \(w_1 + w_{12} = 0\) (neuron 1, \(x_2 = 0, 1\));
    • \(w_2 = 0\) and \(w_2 + w_{12} = 0\) (neuron 2, \(x_1 = 0, 1\)).
  • \(2^4 = 16\) sign patterns. Two of them give cyclic state diagrams, e.g. \(00 \to 01 \to 11 \to 10 \to 00\), which no energy allows.
  • The other \(14\) are exactly the \(14\) chambers: every acyclic dynamics of two neurons is realized.

Storing Data Is a Cone

  • The parameters that store the data, with every data point a strict local minimum, are \[ \mathcal{C}_Q = \big\{ W : \Delta_i(x) < 0 \text{ for all } x \in \mathrm{supp}\, \pi^Q \text{ and all } i \big\}. \]
  • \(\mathcal{C}_Q\) is an open polyhedral cone, a union of chambers, and the negative set of tropical MEF.
  • MEF drives \(W\) to infinity inside \(\mathcal{C}_Q\). It selects a direction, not a point.
  • So which directions in \(\mathcal{C}_Q\) are good, and how large is \(\mathcal{C}_Q\)?

Stability

  • The arrangement is central: every hyperplane \(\{h_i(x) = 0\}\) passes through \(W = 0\). So each chamber \(C\) is an open cone, \(W \in C \Rightarrow rW \in C\) for \(r > 0\), and the dynamics depend only on the direction \(u = W / \lVert W \rVert\).
  • Stability. If \(W \in C\), any perturbation \(W + \eta\) with \(\lVert \eta \rVert\) less than the distance from \(W\) to the walls of \(C\) keeps the same dynamics. A chamber is stable if most of its directions are far from its walls.
  • Solid angle. Measure the size of \(C\) by \[ \omega(C) = \frac{\mathrm{vol}(C \cap B)}{\mathrm{vol}(B)} = \mathbb{P}\big(u \in C\big) = \mathbb{P}\big(W \in C\big), \] for \(B\) the unit ball, \(u\) a uniform random direction, or \(W\) a standard Gaussian.
  • The solid angles of all chambers sum to \(1\): \(\omega(C)\) is the chance that a random network has the dynamics of \(C\).

Projection Volumes

  • For a cone \(C \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\), project a Gaussian vector onto \(C\). Let \(v_k(C)\) be the probability that the projection lands in the relative interior of a \(k\)-dimensional face. Then \(\sum_k v_k(C) = 1\), \(v_n(C) = \omega(C)\), and \(v_0(C)\) is the solid angle of the polar cone.
  • Theorem (Klivans-Swartz; conjectured by Drton-Klivans for reflection arrangements). For a central arrangement with characteristic polynomial \(\chi(t) = \sum_k a_k t^k\), \[ \sum_{C \text{ chamber}} v_k(C) = \lvert a_k \rvert. \]
  • So the average solid angle is \(1 / \#\text{chambers}\), and the finer projection volumes are counted by \(\chi\). Two neurons: the sums over chambers are \(1, 4, 6, 3\) for \(k = 3, 2, 1, 0\), matching \(\chi(t) = t^3 - 4t^2 + 6t - 3\) [numerical].
  • The distribution of solid angles across chambers, and \(\chi\) for all \(d\), are [open].

Singular Learning Theory

Tubular Neighborhoods

  • Singular learning theory. Let \(K \geq 0\) be analytic, with zeros inside the compact support of a prior \(\varphi > 0\). The prior volume of the tubular neighborhood \(\{K < \varepsilon\}\) satisfies \[ V(\varepsilon) = \int_{K(W) < \varepsilon} \varphi(W)\, dW = c\, \varepsilon^{\lambda} \big(\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\big)^{m-1}\big(1 + o(1)\big) \qquad (\varepsilon \to 0), \] where \(\lambda\) is the learning coefficient (real log canonical threshold) and \(m\) its multiplicity.
  • So the learning coefficient is the limit \[ \lambda = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{\log V(\varepsilon)}{\log \varepsilon}, \qquad \log V(\tfrac1n) = -\lambda \log n + (m-1)\log\log n + O(1). \]
  • For MEF, \(K_1 > 0\) everywhere and \(K_1 \to 0\) only at infinity, inside the cone \(\mathcal{C}_Q\). The hypotheses fail, and the limit behaves differently.

Watanabe. Algebraic Geometry and Statistical Learning Theory. Cambridge University Press, 2009 (check: the theorem number for the volume asymptotics).

Volumes at Infinity

  • Write \(W = r u\) with \(\lVert u \rVert = 1\), and let \(c(u) = -\max_{x,\,i}\ \tfrac12\Delta_i(x)\) at \(u\), over \(x \in \mathrm{supp}\,\pi^Q\): minus tropical MEF, positive exactly on \(\mathcal{C}_Q\).
  • Since \(\pi^Q_{\min}\, e^{-r c(u)} \leq K_1(r u) \leq d\, e^{-r c(u)}\), with \(L = \log\frac1\varepsilon\), \[ \{K_1 < \varepsilon\} \approx \{ r u : u \in \mathcal{C}_Q,\ r\, c(u) > L \}. \]
  • Heavy-tailed prior, radial density \(\propto r^{-1-a}\) [open]: \(V(\varepsilon) \approx C\, L^{-a}\) with \(C \propto \int_{\mathcal{C}_Q} c(u)^a\, du\) over unit directions, a solid angle weighted by depth. Then \(\log V(\varepsilon) / \log \varepsilon \to 0\): the limit gives \(\lambda = 0\).
  • Standard Gaussian prior [open]: \(\log V(\varepsilon) \approx -\dfrac{L^2}{2\gamma^2}\), with margin \(\gamma = \max_u c(u)\), a hard-margin SVM problem. Then \(\log V(\varepsilon)/\log\varepsilon \approx L/(2\gamma^2) \to \infty\): no finite \(\lambda\), and the right scale is \((\log \varepsilon)^2\), with coefficient \(1/(2\gamma^2)\).

Temperature as a Coordinate

  • Fixed temperature. On the unit sphere, \(u \mapsto K_1(\beta u)\) is generically regular: \(\lambda = (D-1)/2\) with \(D = 2^d - 1\), blind to the data [open].
  • Keep the temperature as \(t = e^{-\beta} = 1/b\), the tropical base. In coordinates \((u, t)\), \[ K(u, t) = \sum_k \pi_k\, t^{c_k(u)}, \qquad c_k(u) = -\tfrac12 \Delta_i(x)(u), \] and the zero-temperature truth sits at the boundary \(t = 0\).
  • With prior \(\beta \sim \mathrm{Exp}(h)\) (density \(\propto t^{h-1}\)) and a smooth prior on directions, \(\lambda = h/\gamma\) [numerical], where \(\gamma = \max_{\lVert u \rVert = 1} \min_k c_k(u)\) is the margin of \(\mathcal{C}_Q\).
  • \(K\) is not analytic at \(t = 0\), so Watanabe’s theorem does not apply as stated; the log power in \(V(\varepsilon)\) is [open].

Margins and Solid Angles

  • Two neurons, \(\beta \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)\), uniform directions:
data margin \(\gamma\) \(1/\gamma\) fitted \(\lambda\) solid angle of \(\mathcal{C}_Q\)
\(\{00\}\) \(1/(2\sqrt2)\) \(2.828\) \(2.838\) \(1/4\)
\(\{00, 11\}\) \(1/(2\sqrt6)\) \(4.899\) \(4.909\) \(1/24\)
  • Two memories are more complex than one: smaller margin, smaller solid angle, larger \(\lambda\).
  • \(\gamma\) is a weighted angular inradius: a cap of angular radius about \(\gamma\) fits inside \(\mathcal{C}_Q\). So a small solid angle forces a small margin and a large \(\lambda\).
  • Not conversely: a long thin cone can have a sizeable solid angle and a tiny margin. \(\lambda\) sees the depth of the cone; the solid angle only bounds it.
  • In \(V(\varepsilon) \approx C\, \varepsilon^{h/\gamma} (\log\frac1\varepsilon)^{-k}\), the rate comes from the margin, and \(C\) and \(k\) from the local shape of the cone at its deepest direction.

Information Cohomology

A Semiring of Refinements

  • A refinement is a surjection \(\pi : Z \to X\) of finite sets, with measures \(q \ll p\) on \(Z\) and their push-forwards on \(X\). Think of \(Z\) as a finer observable than \(X\).
  • Sum: the disjoint union \(\pi_1 + \pi_2 : Z_1 + Z_2 \to X_1 + X_2\), with \(q_1 + q_2\) and \(p_1 + p_2\).
  • Product: the Cartesian product \(\pi_1 \times \pi_2 : Z_1 \times Z_2 \to X_1 \times X_2\), with product measures \(q_1 \times q_2\) and \(p_1 \times p_2\).
  • Up to isomorphism, refinements form a commutative semiring \(\mathcal{S}\): zero is the empty map, one is the identity of a point with masses \((1, 1)\), and products distribute over sums.
  • Example: forgetting neuron \(d\), \(\{0,1\}^d \to \{0,1\}^{d-1}\), is a refinement. So is \(\{0,1\}^d \to \bullet\), which forgets everything.

Dual Numbers

  • The semiring of dual numbers is \(\mathcal{R} = \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\varepsilon] / \langle \varepsilon^2 \rangle\), with \[ \begin{aligned} (a + b\varepsilon) + (c + d\varepsilon) &= (a + c) + (b + d)\varepsilon, \\ (a + b\varepsilon)(c + d\varepsilon) &= ac + (ad + bc)\varepsilon. \end{aligned} \]
  • A map \(\pi \mapsto a(\pi) + b(\pi)\varepsilon\) is a semiring homomorphism if and only if \(a\) is a homomorphism and \(b\) is a derivation over \(a\): \[ b(\pi_1 + \pi_2) = b(\pi_1) + b(\pi_2), \qquad b(\pi_1 \times \pi_2) = a(\pi_2)\, b(\pi_1) + a(\pi_1)\, b(\pi_2). \]
  • Tangent addition \((a + b\varepsilon) \circ (a + c\varepsilon) = a + (b + c)\varepsilon\) makes \(\mathcal{R}\) a category: objects \(a \geq 0\), morphisms the loops \(a + b\varepsilon\) at \(a\).

Relative Information as a Semiring Homomorphism

  • For a refinement \(\pi : Z \to X\), the conditional relative information is \[ I_{q\Vert p}(Z \,\vert\, X) = \sum_{x} q(x) \sum_{z \in \pi^{-1}(x)} q(z \vert x) \log\frac{q(z\vert x)}{p(z \vert x)} \;\geq\; 0. \]
  • Theorem. \(\mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{R}\), \(\ \pi \mapsto T_q + I_{q\Vert p}(Z \,\vert\, X)\,\varepsilon\), is a semiring homomorphism:
    • sum rule: \(I(\pi_1 + \pi_2) = I(\pi_1) + I(\pi_2)\);
    • product rule: \(I(\pi_1 \times \pi_2) = T_2\, I(\pi_1) + T_1\, I(\pi_2)\), the Leibniz rule.
  • Absolute relative information is the refinement \(Z \to \bullet\). The sum of \(Z_1 \to \bullet\) and \(Z_2 \to \bullet\) lands in two points, which is why \(I(Z)\) alone is additive only when \(T_q = T_p\).

Lin. Relative Information Theory I: Information Cohomology, §§2.1-2.3 (in preparation).

Relative Information as a Functor

  • Refinements also compose: \(Z \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } X \xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ } Y\).
  • With \(F(\pi) = T_q + I_{q\Vert p}(Z \,\vert\, X)\,\varepsilon\), the chain rule \[ I(Z \,\vert\, Y) = I(Z \,\vert\, X) + I(X \,\vert\, Y) \] says \(F(\sigma \circ \pi) = F(\sigma) \circ F(\pi)\) under tangent addition, and identities map to \(T_q + 0\,\varepsilon\).
  • So \(F\) is a functor that respects sums and products: relative information is a derivation over total measure, a 1-cocycle.
  • Uniqueness. The measurable \(h(q, p)\) with \(h(q_1q_2, p_1p_2) = q_1 h(q_2, p_2) + h(q_1, p_1)\, q_2\) are \(q(a\log q + b\log p)\); those vanishing on the diagonal are the multiples of \(q\log\frac{q}{p}\).

Conclusion

Summary

  • A binary flip process is an action-driven process on \(\{0,1\}^d\) with one flip action per neuron and rates \(e^{-\beta E(x, y)}\), reversible for the Gibbs law.
  • At zero temperature it becomes a deterministic descent to the local minima of \(E\), with the same fixed points as a Hopfield network.
  • The relative information rate between a true and a model process becomes minimum energy flow when the true transition rates out of the data are zero.
  • Zero temperature is the tropical limit \(b = e^\beta \to \infty\). Tropical MEF is a maximum of linear forms whose negative set is the cone of parameters storing the data.
  • That cone is a union of chambers of a hyperplane arrangement, whose geometry measures stability.
  • Total measure and relative information form a semiring homomorphism into the dual numbers: relative information is a derivation, a 1-cocycle.
